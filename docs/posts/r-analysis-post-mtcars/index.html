Do Manual Cars Really Pack More Horsepower?

Analysis with: R (dplyr, ggplot2), Quarto

Author

Ahmed Muhaimin Choudhury

Published

September 25, 2026

Data: mtcars, extracted from the 1974 Motor Trend US magazine and bundled with base R. See ?mtcars for the full variable list. Public domain.

For ages, stick car people have taunted the automatic car users like they don’t qualify to be on their level. And no one actually questioned, who’s faster? Who packs more horsepower? I am looking for an answer to that question today. Today, is the day of revelation.

Data

# setup
library(dplyr)
library(ggplot2)

# recode transmission from 0/1 to readable labels
cars <- mtcars |>
  mutate(transmission = if_else(am == 0, "Automatic", "Manual"))

The mtcars dataset records fuel consumption and ten aspects of automobile design and performance for 32 cars from the 1973–74 model year. This post asks one question: Do manual-transmission cars tend to have more horsepower than automatic ones?

Summary

library(knitr)

cars |>
  group_by(transmission) |>
  summarise(
    n = n(),
    mean_hp = round(mean(hp), 1),
    median_hp = round(median(hp), 1),
    sd_hp = round(sd(hp), 1),
    min_hp = min(hp),
    max_hp = max(hp)
  ) |> 
  kable()

Automatic cars in this dataset actually have a higher mean horsepower than manual cars, but the standard deviation is large and the sample size is small. The distribution matters more than the mean.

Visualization

ggplot(cars, aes(x = hp, fill = transmission)) +
  geom_density(alpha = 0.5) +
  labs(
    x = "Horsepower",
    y = "Density",
    fill = "Transmission"
  ) +
  theme_minimal()

Inference

The density plot tells a more nuanced story than the summary means. Manual cars concentrate in the 60–130 hp range, whereas automatics are more spread out and include a heavier right tail up past 300 hp. The perception that “manuals are always sportier” doesn’t hold in this dataset as it’s the automatics that carry the high-horsepower muscle cars of the mid-1970s. A larger, more modern sample would probably flip the story, but for 1974 Motor Trend cars, automatic transmission and high horsepower go together more often than not.
